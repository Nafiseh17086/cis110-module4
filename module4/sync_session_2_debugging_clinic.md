## The three questions to ask before asking anyone for help

Answer these **before** you post in Q&A, email, or ask an AI assistant. Half the
time, answering them *is* the fix.

1. **What did I expect, and what actually happened?** Write both down exactly:
   the exact output, or the exact last line of the error message, not "it doesn't
   work".
2. **What is the smallest input that still shows the problem?** Cut
   `SAMPLE_TEXT` down to one sentence, then to three words. A bug you can show
   with `"a b a"` is a bug you can trace by hand.
3. **What have I already checked, and what did it rule out?** "I printed
   `words` right after `get_words` and it was correct, so the problem is after
   that line."

## How to prepare

1. **Pick one real problem** from Lab 4: a wrong answer, a crash, or a self-check
   line that stays NOT YET. It is fine if you have since fixed it, since the
   broken version is what we need.
2. **Fill in the [submission form](debugging_clinic_submission_form.md)** and
   submit it on Canvas **at least 48 hours before** the session.
3. **Submissions are anonymized before they are shown.** Your name and anything
   identifying are removed, and nobody will be asked to present their own code.

## What we will *not* do

- We will not share complete Lab 4 solutions. We fix the specific problem, and
  you finish the lab.
- We will not judge anyone's code. Every experienced programmer has written all
  of these bugs. That is how they recognize them.

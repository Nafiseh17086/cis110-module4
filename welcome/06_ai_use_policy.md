# CIS 110 AI-Use Policy

**This is required reading.** Complete the acknowledgment quiz on Canvas after
reading it; Module 1 opens once you have.

---

## Why this course has a policy at all

AI assistants can now write working code in seconds. That is genuinely useful,
and you will learn to use them well. But code that *looks* right and code that
*is* right are not the same thing, and the only person who can tell the
difference is someone who can read and write code themselves.

So this course builds your own ability first, then adds AI step by step.

## The four permission levels

**Every lab and assignment is labeled with exactly one of these levels.** The
label is at the top of the instructions. If you are ever unsure, ask before you
start — never after.

| Level | What you may do | Where you'll see it |
|---|---|---|
| **Unassisted** | No AI tools at all — not for writing code, and not for explanations. Readings, Python documentation, and the Q&A discussion are fine. | Modules 1–2, both checkpoints |
| **Assistance for Explanation Only** | You may ask an AI to *explain* code or concepts. You may **not** ask it to write, complete, or fix code. | Module 3 |
| **Assistance Permitted with Disclosure** | You may use AI for code, but you must write your own working version first where the instructions say so, and record everything in your AI-Use Reflection. | Modules 4–6, 8–9 |
| **Assistance Expected** | Using AI is part of the assignment. You are graded on how well you specify, evaluate, and document — not just on the final code. | Module 7, parts of the Final Project |

## What stays the same at every level

1. **You are responsible for every line you submit**, whoever or whatever wrote it.
   "The AI wrote that" is never an explanation for code you cannot explain.
2. **Every lab is followed by an AI-Use Reflection**, even when the honest answer
   is "none". See [How to Complete an AI-Use Reflection](08_how_to_complete_an_ai_use_reflection.md).
3. **Undisclosed AI use is an academic-integrity violation**, even at levels where
   AI is allowed. Disclosure is what makes the use legitimate.

## What happens if you make a mistake

If you realize you used AI beyond what an assignment allowed, tell your
instructor before it is graded. An honest disclosure is treated very differently
from undisclosed use that is discovered later.
